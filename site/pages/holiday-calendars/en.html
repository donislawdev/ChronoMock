Holiday calendars

Which days are working days, for counting business days and for the weekend and holiday marks on a result. {{calendar_count}} calendars come with the program.

What a calendar does

A calendar knows the weekend and the public holidays of one place. With one picked, the calculator can:

  • count business days: --shift +5bd skips weekends and holidays,
  • move to the nearest business day with --nearest nbd or pbd,
  • say of the result whether it is a business day, and which holiday it is.

In the window, pick one in the Calendar field of the Calculator. On the command line it is chrono calc --calendar with the name to type. A scenario made for one market counts in that market's calendar by itself. A session uses a calendar only to work out the date it starts at, from a scenario that counts business days. 

chrono calc --base 2026-12-22T09:00:00 --shift +3bd --calendar pl
Chrono Mock - date calculator
  base:    2026-12-22T09:00:00
  step 1:  shift +3 business_days  -> 2026-12-29T09:00:00
  result:  2026-12-29T09:00:00
  ...
    business day  yes  (pl)
    holiday       no

Three business days from Tuesday 22 December end on the 29th: in Poland Christmas Eve, both days of Christmas and the weekend between them are off.

The years a calendar covers

Holidays change: a law adds one, another moves. So each calendar says from which year it is sure, and on which day its holidays were last checked against the law.

  • Before its first year a business-day step is refused with a message, and a date there is shown as not judged rather than as a working day.
  • After the day it was checked the calendar goes on with the law of that day. Once that day is more than a year ago, a result after it is marked as possibly out of date, because a holiday may have been added since.

A holiday on a weekend

Some places give a day off in place of a holiday that falls on a Saturday or a Sunday, and each calendar below says which day that is. When that day is off already, the day off moves on to the next working day in the same direction.

The calendars

chrono calendars lists the same ones, with any you add. Each holiday is listed with the years it applies in, within the years the calendar covers.

Your own calendar

A calendar holds every day off of its place by itself: it cannot add to another one. To count your company's own days off too, copy the calendar of your country and add them to the copy.

  1. Copy pl.json from the folder chrono calendars names on its second line, for example to pl-office.json, and set id in it to pl-office. The file name without .json must be the same as id.
  2. Add each day to holidays, shaped like the one below, with a source that says where it comes from. A holiday without a source is refused, because people count payment dates from these files.
  3. Run chrono calendars again. Your calendar is in the list, or among the files left out, with the reason. From then on --calendar pl-office counts with it.

What the file holds

KeyWhat it is
schemaAlways chronomock.calendar/1.
idThe name to type, the same as the file name.
country, subregionThe country in two letters, such as PL, and null.
nameen, and local in the place's own language.
weekendThe days off every week, such as ["saturday", "sunday"].
observedWhich day is off for a holiday on a weekend: none, sat_to_fri_sun_to_mon, sun_to_mon or weekend_to_mon. Anything but none needs a Saturday and Sunday weekend.
valid_fromThe first year everything in the calendar has a source for: the holidays, the weekend and the rule for weekends.
law_as_ofThe day the holidays were checked against the law, written YYYY-MM-DD.
sourceWhere the first year, the weekend and the rule for weekends come from.
holidaysEach with an id, a name, the rule for its date, the years valid_from and valid_to (null for no limit) and its own source.

A key the program does not know is refused with its name, so a typo cannot drop a holiday without a word.

The date of a holiday

ruleThe day
{ "type": "fixed", "month": 11, "day": 11 }The same date every year: 11 November.
{ "type": "nth_weekday", "month": 1, "weekday": "monday", "order": 3 }The third Monday of January. order is 1 to 5, or -1 for the last one of the month.
{ "type": "easter_offset", "offset": 60 }60 days after Easter Sunday. 0 is Easter Sunday itself, and a minus counts back. From -80 to 250, which keeps the day in the year of its Easter.
